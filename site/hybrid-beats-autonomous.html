The validation gate

My initial mental model for AI agents was that they "just do the processing". In a way, the naive idea is that you just give the agent the task, and then it gets the task done. Well well. I haven't seen many of those agents. Simply because that technology is not ready yet — nor will be ready anytime soon.

The principle: Hybrid beats autonomous — the move that scales is not "remove the human," it's "put the human in the one place that matters."

Think of the run like a canal lock: water rises and falls freely on either side, but there is one chamber where the level changes for good, and that is where the gate sits. Find the step in the agent run that can't be undone, gate exactly there — and let the run flow everywhere else.

Personally it's the discipline that lets you ship an agent without betting what you can't take back; at company scope it's the architecture that decides where autonomy is allowed to touch the irreversible — the org-level guardrail every customer-facing, money-moving, data-touching deployment composes against, and the move that turns "should we automate this?" from a vibe into a placed gate.

Drop the lock now — the rest is plain agent plumbing. A run has steps: it drafts, it classifies, it routes, it sends. Most of those steps are reversible — a bad draft gets re-drafted, a wrong classification gets re-routed, and nothing left the building. Then there's the one step where the level changes for good: the refund wired, the email sent, the record updated, the migration committed. The gate is not about how good the model is. It's about which step can't be undone. Same agent, same accuracy — a 5% error rate is fine on a step you can roll back and catastrophic on a step you can't.

Mark the run

List the steps; tag each reversible or not.

Most teams never do this — the demo blurred the steps into one autonomous blur. Writing them out is the whole analysis. Beside each step in the run, reversible or not, honest about which actions actually reach a customer, move money, or change a record you can't roll back. The first not is where the level changes for good.

Place the gate

Put a human exactly on the irreversibility line — and nowhere else.

Let the run generate at full speed up to that step. Then one human: approve, reject, modify. Not a reviewer reading everything — that's just slow autonomy. The gate is cheap: a named approver, a one-step sign-off in a chat thread, a named owner in a doc. The artefact is the placed gate, not the agent. Past it, the run executes; execution is mechanical once approved.

Watch the level

Keep a human monitoring — the line moves.

The irreversibility line isn't fixed forever. A step that needed approval when the agent was new might, after a thousand verified runs without incident, be reclassifiable as safe to automate — or a near-miss might reveal a step you'd mis-tagged as reversible. Every escalation is a flag on a step; every near-miss is data, not bad luck. The step map is a living document: review it when the workflow changes, when the model updates, when volume jumps an order of magnitude. That's how the line gets re-drawn on evidence rather than optimism — and the re-tagging is exactly the operational context a compounding system is built to keep, so the next placement is faster and more grounded than this one.

The trick is finding ways to do more of the input-giving that adds the most value. Then diminish the baby-sitting and mindless prodding the agent along.

The honest part · where the line is still moving

The claim is real — and so is the edge it stops at.

I won't sell you "gates always win." The named reversals that make this vivid — Zillow's algorithmic home-buying programme ($500M write-down), Arup's fraudulent ~$25M wire, Air Canada's tribunal-enforced hallucinated fare policy — are illustrative examples drawn from a convergence finding (15+ independent signals across 10 domains, assembled March 2026; practitioner reports, vendor reversals, consumer studies, analyst predictions). They aren't proof on their own; the evidence is the convergence. And the counter-evidence travels with the claim: a minority of practitioners — around 22% in one survey — report full-replacement success in bounded, well-scoped domains, and some customer-service deployments match human satisfaction (WeightWatchers cited at 4.6/5, SeatGeek at comparable CSAT).

So here's what I can't yet tell you: where exactly your irreversibility line sits — and it won't sit still. The December 2025 coding shift moved it; the next model release will move it again.

That's not a hole in the move — it is the move. Hybrid beats autonomous holds at the high-stakes, high-volume, irreversible end; it is not a law of nature that gates always outperform. Refusing to fake the number is what keeps the gate placement honest, because the only way to find your line is to mark your own run and watch what the monitor surfaces. The deliverable isn't a fixed answer. It's a placed gate you keep re-checking.

Convergent evidence as of the March 2026 finding — not a controlled result. The 85%-per-step / ~20%-over-10-steps figure used elsewhere is an illustrative calculation of how errors compound down a chain, not a measured benchmark.

Some human-in-the-loop is real. It will not go away. Even when I’m foreseeing the disruption of careers as they are, I’m thinking that the human activity will stay in businesses. Humans connect the digital stuff into reality. The human binds the imaginary stuff that Ai generates into reality. Humans curate, in a way, what is real and what is fabulation.

“Selling agents?” · Jan 2026
Monday

Take one workflow you've already pointed an agent at. Write its steps in a line — the literal run, step by step. Beside each, write reversible or not, honest about which actions actually reach a customer, move money, or change a record you can't roll back. Circle the first not. That circle is your lock — the one chamber where the level changes for good. Name who approves it and what would make them say no. One list, one circle, ten minutes — and you've turned "can we just let it run?" from a vibe into a step you can point at.
